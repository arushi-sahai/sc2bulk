A benchmark of deep learning sample representations from scRNA‑seq for predicting phenotypes in bulk RNA‑seq cohorts

Arushi Sahai1, Beatriz Campillo3, Luvna Dhawka2,3, Natalie Stanley1,2,4

1Department of Computer Science, 2Computational Medicine Program, 3Curriculum in Bioinformatics and Computational Biology, 4Department of Genetics, The University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill

Introduction

  • Motivation: Phenotype models trained on single-cell RNA‑seq capture cell-level biology, but single-cell cohorts are small; applying such models to larger bulk RNA‑seq cohorts, which often carry clinical outcomes, would allow the models to be validated and used at scale.
  • Challenge: Single-cell and bulk cohorts often differ in how the tissue was prepared and originate from different individuals, so a model trained on single-cell samples may not generalize to bulk samples.
  • Approach: The simplest representation of a single-cell sample is pseudobulk, a fixed sum of the sample's cells; deep learning models instead learn a nonlinear representation from the individual cells, and foundation models also add pretraining on millions of cells.
  • Question: Do deep learning sample representations of single-cell samples predict bulk phenotypes more accurately than pseudobulk?

Benchmark design

  • Task: A ridge classifier learns the phenotype from representations of labeled single-cell samples and is then applied unchanged to bulk samples, scored by bulk AUROC from 0.5 (chance) to 1 (perfect) (a).
  • Comparison: The classifier receives pseudobulk or one of nine deep learning representations from three model families (b, c); differences in bulk AUROC from pseudobulk measure each representation's quality.
  • Datasets: Six datasets of paired single-cell and bulk cohorts differ in tissue preparation, phenotype definition, and cohort independence (d, e).

No deep learning sample representation outperformed pseudobulk

  • Reading the plot: Each point is a representation's bulk AUROC minus that of pseudobulk; points left of zero indicate lower accuracy (a).
  • Averaging: A single dataset is too small to detect a 0.05 AUROC difference, the smallest advantage we consider worth the training, tuning, and GPU a deep learning representation requires; we therefore average over the four datasets marked ♦, excluding the two where pseudobulk's AUROC of 0.95 or more makes a 0.05 advantage impossible.
  • Main result: Averaged over the four ♦ datasets, pseudobulk significantly outperformed seven of the nine deep learning representations and did not differ significantly from either CytoSet representation; no representation significantly outperformed pseudobulk in any dataset (a).
  • Baselines and controls: Permuting the single-cell labels reduced pseudobulk to chance, an untrained CytoSet and pseudobulk reduced to 64 principal components scored below pseudobulk, and a ridge classifier trained and tested within the bulk samples scored above pseudobulk (a).
  • Learning versus transfer: Every representation predicted the phenotype accurately within single-cell data, so the models themselves are sound; the deficit relative to pseudobulk arises in the transfer to bulk samples (b).
  • Cells or pseudobulk as input: CytoSet, Geneformer, and scGPT can also encode a sample's pseudobulk instead of individual cells. With either input, CytoSet scored close to pseudobulk itself, whereas the frozen foundation models scored well below pseudobulk (c).

Batch correction did not improve transfer

  • Rationale: Single-cell and bulk cohorts differ systematically, in part because of technical factors such as tissue preparation; we tested whether removing such differences between assays improves transfer.
  • Methods: We compared no correction (across-assay standard scaling), mean and variance correction (within-assay standard scaling), and batch correction (ComBat or Harmony), each applied without phenotype labels.
  • Reading the plot: Panel (a) plots samples on the first two principal components. The silhouette ranges from 0 (assays mixed) to 1 (assays separate). Panels (b) and (c) show the silhouette and bulk AUROC after each correction method is applied to the sample representations.
  • Separation: Within-assay scaling, our default method, removed most of the difference between assays in every dataset; ComBat further reduced the difference slightly, and Harmony partly restored the difference (b).
  • Transfer: Bulk AUROC did not improve as the difference between assays was reduced, and CytoSet and scGPT stayed below pseudobulk (c).

Conclusions

  • Pseudobulk, which requires no training, matched or outperformed every representation tested, establishing pseudobulk as the baseline any new deep learning representation must outperform.
  • The benchmark and code are public for evaluating new representations.
